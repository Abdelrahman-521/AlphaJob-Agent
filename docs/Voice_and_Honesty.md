# Voice and Honesty: how everything Abdel sends must read

> Open this before you write any letter, resume summary, email or LinkedIn message. Facts come from `Profile_Facts.md`; this file owns how to say them. Procedure (who to message, when, follow-ups) lives in `Start_Here.md` OUTREACH.

## 1. Honesty (hard rules)
- **Never invent:** no job, title, date, tool he didn't use, number, award or company.
- **Word bending is allowed, up to about 20% stretch in framing.** Describe true experience in the posting's own words and sell transferable skills hard. Sport Chek floor work becomes "client-facing problem solving"; interpreting becomes "translating needs between stakeholders". If you're unsure, say it plainly.
- **Say the gap.** Every letter names one thing he hasn't done yet ("I haven't held a SaaS support title yet, but…"). Honest gaps read as confidence.
- **Fixed wordings:**
  - **Sport Chek:** close-knit sales team at a store that had to fight for every sale. He led the store in loyalty sign-ups and was being trained as a manager (survey metrics, cycle counts, hiring). Never "top salesperson" or "hit the daily budget".
  - **Behavior cloning:** the course supplied the simulator, expert controller and logger. He implemented the PyTorch training loop, captured the data and tested the model. Never "built the full pipeline".
  - **Team projects:** the RdRp/Nextflow pipeline and the game server were team projects. Say "on a team". His part of the server was the API.
  - **Management:** about 1 year of leadership. Never the manager title.
  - **Courses:** "in progress". **"Learning" vs "curious about":** only say "learning X" if he really is (UiPath, Claude Code). For K8s, AWS, Docker and the like, say "curious about".
  - **Retail and POS:** he worked a point-of-sale on busy floors. Never "the one everyone came to when the system broke" or any invented support role.
- **The agent:** when a message mentions it, never hide that an AI agent helped. Never claim Abdel built Claude or wrote code he didn't. Never claim to be human. A direct "was AI used?" question goes to Abdel (`Start_Here.md` AI_TRIPWIRE).

## 2. Style (everything he sends)
- Plain and human. Few em dashes and commas. Short sentences. No corporate filler.
- **Banned phrases:** "exactly where/what I want", "I am writing to express my interest", "perfect fit", "passionate about leveraging", "top 20 company", and any company flattery.
- **Humour:** one light, genuine line is welcome, especially with guys. Examples: "a quick hello beats being one more PDF in the pile", "the friend everyone calls when their laptop acts up", "'it's just a little hill' is the most dangerous sentence in Newfoundland".
- **Personal hook first:** a real reason (German cars, police / 911, running, the brother in RPA, home in Newfoundland, business courses alongside CS for banking roles). Then fit, then drive (door to door, varsity track, two 50K ultras, "I'm a fighter").
- **Model letter for non-tech roles:** a hand-written service-manager letter in the private build.js (warm reason first, then leadership and fight). Tech and national roles use the generator (section 3).

## 3. Cover letters (generated by `natLetter` in build.js from `national.json`)
- One page. The structure is fixed by the builder:
  1. Hook paragraph (custom per job): a real personal reason plus one or two posting details.
  2. Track paragraph (dev / support / data / impl / ai / sales / service / admin), already written.
  3. "Things a lot of new grads don't" paragraph (sales, Walmart training).
  4. Gap + fighter + relocation close.
- **You write per job:** `hook`, `gap`, `summary` (the resume summary, packed with the posting's exact keywords), `track`, `resume`.
- **Resume variants:** `techDev` (no Walmart; dev and AI roles) · `tech` (with Walmart; IT support, systems, implementation) · `data` (data and BA roles) · `ai` (AI roles) · `sales` (SDR/BDR/inside sales) · `client` (customer service, admin, banking front line) · `mgmt` (store and department management).
- **Keyword check:** target 85 to 90%+ on `ats_check.py` (raised from 70% by Abdel, Oct 8). Add missing terms only where they're true, in the posting's own words. It's a target, not a limiter: never skip or delay an application because the honest score lands lower.
- **Sales and finance applications (Abdel, Oct 8):** say briefly that he learned the fundamentals of door-to-door selling from Sales Superstar, and that he took economics and business courses alongside his degree. One short sentence each. Never call Sales Superstar a podcast, a course or a certification. The `sales` and `finance` letter tracks already carry both lines, and sales, client and manager resumes get an "economics and business coursework" line under Education.
- **EDGE jobs** add the P.S. in section 5.

## 4. Recruiter and hiring-manager messages (normal voice)
- **Subject (Abdel, Oct 7):** starts with "Applied:", e.g. "Applied: Technical Support Specialist, XXXX".
- **Length:** under 120 words for a message, 300 characters for a connect note (Premium: unlimited notes, 15 InMails a month).
- **Must contain:** their name, the exact role, one real detail from the posting, one honest gap or proof line, the city he's ready to move to, and a light ask ("would love 10 minutes", "a pointer to who is, if it isn't you").
- **Sign-off:** "Thanks, Abdelrahman (Abdel) Conber".
- **Tone (Abdel, Oct 3):** don't bother people. Smooth, concise, genuine, professional. One message, then wait. A thank-you after they accept is fine.
- **Model A (hiring manager, construction company, Oct 5):**
  > Hi XXXX, I just applied for the Construction Innovation Systems Analyst role on your team. I write Python and SQL, build APIs, and prototype with Claude Code every day, always with a human check on what it produces. Right now I'm also working through UiPath agentic automation, and automation runs in the family since my brother is an RPA consultant in Sydney. ## 6. Emails from his MUN Gmail
- Job application emails need no OK: send them and report after. Any other email needs his OK first.
- Subject: "Application: [Role] ([City]) - Abdelrahman Conber". Attach the resume and letter PDFs, and check that both attached.
- Body: 4 to 6 short lines, the same voice as section 4.
- **Tailor every application email (Abdel, Oct 8: "Be very tailored. Be human. Be genuine.").** A generic "MUN grad with business courses, can start right away" body is not enough. In short, gentle paragraphs:
  - Pick up 2 or 3 of the posting's own keywords and say how he does them (e.g. admin: phones, scheduling, filing, MS Office, data entry; restaurant: inventory, staff, suppliers).
  - Sport Chek: a year and a half on the sales floor, trained as a manager, helped with hiring.
  - Good with computers (CS degree; the person who sorts out the tech).
  - He wants to take the next step, is open to opportunities and learns fast.
  - Close warm: lives in St. John's, can start right away, open work permit, would love to chat.
  - Not long. The 5 emails sent before this rule (five local NL employers) used the generic body; they're sent, so leave them.
- **Sound human, never like a form (Abdel, Oct 5).** When a posting asks you to address points (company values, questions), weave them into normal sentences. No labelled lists like "We Care: ...". Model: a CRM-consultancy email (Oct 5): "Your posting asked how I fit your values, so here's the honest version..." then 3 short paragraphs of plain talk.

## 7. Written answers on forms
- Short-answer questions ("why us", "tell us about a time"): 3 to 6 sentences in his voice, one real example, the honest gap if it's relevant. Same banned phrases.
- Character limits: Eightfold 499, LinkedIn note 300. Count before pasting.
- If the form has an AI tripwire, stop and hand it to Abdel (`Start_Here.md` AI_TRIPWIRE).
